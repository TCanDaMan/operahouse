# War Memorial Opera House — interior geometry and renovation history

Research pass, 2026-09-06. Angle: auditorium interior geometry (especially the ceiling),
and what the 1992–97 renovation did that could matter acoustically.

Legend for each entry:
- **[M]** = a *stated measurement* in the source (a number the source asserts).
- **[D]** = a *description* (qualitative, no number, or a number that is clearly rhetorical).
- **[X]** = *derived* in this repo from imagery/drawings, not published anywhere.

### 1.1 Form and material

| Fact | Type | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Ceiling **below the box balcony (over the rear orchestra) has ornamental cast-plaster soffits** | D | HSR 1993 (line ~4766) |
| The plaster ceiling **"sits atop the auditorium like the lid on a pot"**; Loma Prieta **"cracked [it] all around the base"** (John Priest, SF Opera technical director) | D | NYT, 8 Sep 1997, https://www.nytimes.com/1997/09/08/arts/gala-reopening-for-a-san-francisco-landmark-reinforced-for-earthquakes.html (read via Wayback) |
| Journalist's figure: a net was hung "lest plaster fall **130 feet** to the seats below" | D (unreliable — see §4) | NYT 1997, same article |
| The main-house ornamental ceiling **suffered major plaster spall failures** in Loma Prieta; a safety net was in place from 1989 until the 1996 closure | D | HSR 1993 (line ~4770); SFCV https://www.sfcv.org/articles/music-news/rocking-down-unique-musical-memory-lane-sf-opera |

**Reading of the evidence:** the ceiling is *not* a stepped/coved rear ceiling in the way the
current model implies at the rear. The HSR describes (a) an ornamented main ceiling with a
**large recessed oval** over the orchestra, bounded by a gilded plaster cornice, and (b) plain
**flat plaster** ceilings over the balcony tiers, with **recessed octagonal light fixtures** in
the upper balcony ceiling. So the geometry is: ornamented flat field + recessed oval + gilded
cornice at its perimeter; a separate, lower, flat coffered ceiling over the rear/upper balcony.
No source found gives the oval's diameter, its depth, or the ceiling's height above the floor.

### 1932 as-built (The Architect and Engineer, Nov 1932; local text `sources/research/ae1932/AE_1932_text.txt`)
Article "Opera House Stage Largest in America", by Dariel Fitzkee — all **[M]** unless noted.

- Proscenium: **"approximately fifty feet"** soffit to stage floor; **fifty-two feet** side to side.
- Gridiron **116 ft** above the stage; a second gridiron at **125 ft**.
- Stage wall to wall **~135 ft**; back wall **~90 ft** from the proscenium.
- Structural article (C. H. Snyder): stage floor clear of columns **72 ft × 126 ft**; first fly
  galleries **39 ft** above the stage projecting **25 ft** each side; highest fly gallery
  **102 ft**; fly galleries on **72 ft** span plate girders; building **~6,000,000 ft³**,
  **3,800 tons** of structural steel.
- Fire curtain (asbestos and steel) **~10 tons**; **104 sets** of steel cable lines;
  **85 ft** high silver sky cyclorama; switchboard **39 ft** long.
- Four electric stage bridges **4 ft wide**, full proscenium width, descending **11 ft** below
  and rising **8 ft** above the stage; **29 manual traps** in five rows reaching **17 ft**.
- **"the depression of the orchestra fifteen feet below the stage"** — the 1932 pit well depth.
- **The stage "can be built out towards the front two whole rows of seats"** [D] — the forestage
  lift existed from 1932.

### 1965 pit assessment (SOM/WBE report) — all [D] except as noted
- The pit was **the single most-cited deficiency**: it "will scarcely hold seventy musicians,
  one hundred being required at times"; **"the shape is wrong, being too narrow at the ends"**;
  **"the strings are weak and the brass is strong"**.
- **"there is one level only which is at all acoustically acceptable"** — lowering the orchestra
  made it stronger, raising it weaker.
- Recommendation: absorb the movable front strip of the orchestra floor into the pit, costing
  **38 seats for one row, 76 for two** [M].
- HSR 1993: the orchestra pit **retains its original movable, sectioned floor**, plaster walls,
  and **ornamental square coffers below the stage edge** [D].

## 3. AUDITORIUM FORM, SIDE WALLS, BOXES, TIERS

### 3.1 Overall form (HSR 1993, all [D])
- **"one large volume punctuated by three suspended curving balconies"**.
- The three balcony tiers **span wall to wall, cantilevered into the main seating**.
- The **lowest tier at mezzanine level is horseshoe-shaped and carries the box seating**.
- The **dress circle** above has a **front balustrade with intermediate panels hiding recessed
  spot lights**.
- The **uppermost tier** is upper and lower balcony seating with a **simple moulded plaster front**.
- 1932 programme: the auditorium was **built without a single pillar** [D] — but see §4, the HSR
  says cast-stone columns at orchestra level carry the box balcony.

### 3.2 Side walls
| Fact | Type | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Side walls are **rusticated cast-stone below the dress circle**, flat and moulded plaster above and at the rear | D | HSR 1993 |
| **Three large arches with central festooned keystones** punctuate the north and south walls; each arched opening is infilled with a **balustrade, a plaster screen wall and heavy curtains** | D | HSR 1993 |
| 1932: **three well-recessed window openings** with **octagonal and square stone tracery**, backed by wire screen and draped in the stage-curtain fabric, provided **to mask organ pipes** | D | AE Nov 1932 |
| Those openings are the **organ lofts, HSR rooms 233 / 234** on the 2nd floor; the organ was **never installed** and the plaster grilles remain | D | HSR 1993 |

### 5.3 Dates, cost, closure
- Closed at the end of the 1995 season; five months of city structural work, then the company's
  interior work; **18 months total** (NYT). Some sources say the house was dark for the 1996 season.
- Reopened with a gala on **Friday 5 September 1997** (Auerbach) / **6 September 1997** (SFCV).
- Cost: **$84.5M** (Auerbach) / **$88.5M** (NYT) / $86.5M and $90M in later secondary accounts.
  Funding: **$49.5M** city bond + **~$30M** raised by the company + **$7M** from the War Memorial
  board for public amenities + **~$7M** fire insurance.
- Facility is **250,000 sq ft** (SEAONC/Structus).

## 6. THE 1932 ACOUSTIC DESIGN (for context and calibration)

Source: Clifford M. Swan, "The Acoustics of the San Francisco Opera House," *The Architect and
Engineer*, Nov 1932, pp. 45 and 59 — full OCR already in
`sources/research/ae1932/AE_1932_text.txt` (lines ~10130–10250 and ~11640–11670). All [D];
Swan publishes **no numbers**.

- Swan was the consulting acoustical engineer; design was driven by **shape**, decided in advance.
- Schemes were rejected for: **concave curved surfaces** that would focus sound and cause echoes;
  **excessive height or width** that would over-lengthen reverberation and cause delayed
  interfering first reflections; and **deep, low overhanging balconies** that would block sound
  from reaching the back of the recesses.
- The chosen design provided **hard reflecting surfaces of proper extent, contour and location**
  to reinforce the direct sound.
- Reverberation was set **by the heavy absorbent furnishings and the audience**, with **no
  commercial acoustical materials applied**.
- Conclusion (p. 59): well-balanced distribution throughout, "with perfect clearness and
  distinctness, and without loss of quality."

**1932 HVAC (AE Nov 1932, Wm. E. Leland, M.E.)** — all [M]:
- Upward supply/exhaust system with **plenum chambers under the main floor, under the dress
  circle and under the balcony**.
- **Exhaust screens in the ceiling at the rear of the orchestra**, in the box vestibules, and at
  the rear of the dress circle and balcony.
- **Six supply fans, 100,000 CFM total; four exhaust fans, 94,000 CFM** ≈ **30 CFM per seat**.
- ~35 motor-driven units in four fan rooms (two basement, two on the fourth floor).
- 23 stage radiators (2,743 ft²), some **on each side of the arch about 20 ft above stage level**.

**1965 HVAC noise findings (SOM/WBE report)** — all [D]:
- "complaints have been made regarding **fan noise throughout the Auditorium**, particularly the
  stage area."
- Fans and motors mounted on **cork, ineffectual for damping**; ducts isolated from fans by canvas
  bellows but **solidly embedded in walls**, transmitting vibration.
- Recommended: spring isolators and inertia pads under all fans/motors; isolate ductwork from
  walls; **"Treat ducts to plenum over Auditorium acoustically"**; redesign the stage exhaust.
- Separately: **Lower Foyer Bar noise passed through its plaster ceiling into the under-orchestra
  air plenum and out through the orchestra air supply openings**, disturbing patrons at
  intermission — a direct duct-borne path into the orchestra floor.
- 1965 also records **Heinrich Keilholz** designing a demountable acoustical shell for the stage
  with SOM: "the acoustics have been less successful for symphony."

## 7. CONFLICTS

1. **Chandelier diameter: 27 ft (HSR 1993) vs 25 ft (SFGATE, SFCV).** The HSR is the more
   authoritative document type but is a written narrative, not a measured drawing; the 25 ft /
   14 ft pairing appears in two independent journalistic sources and reads like a maintenance
   spec. Unresolved. A stray Facebook claim of "almost 13 feet wide" should be discarded.
2. **Auditorium volume: 776,000 ft³ (1932 opening programme) vs 738,600 ft³ (Beranek 2004).**
   ~5% apart. The 1932 figure may include the stage house or the box-lobby volumes.
3. **Capacity: 3,252 (1965, Beranek) vs 3,302 (HSR 1993) vs 3,146 (published today) vs 3,126
   (SFWMPAC brochure) vs 3,006 (ticketed).** At least three of these are contemporaneous with
   each other, so the published 3,146 is probably a legacy number.
4. **Seats in 1997: restored vs replaced.** Auerbach says the original orchestra seats were
   restored in Canada; the NYT says the construction fire caused all seats to be made new. Both
   sources are contemporaneous and first-hand. This matters because the 2021 project describes
   itself as replacing seats "dating back to 1932", which supports the *restoration* account.
5. **"No pillars" vs cast-stone columns.** The 1932 programme calls the auditorium pillar-free;
   the HSR says cast-stone columns at orchestra level support the box balcony. Both can be true
   if the columns are at the side walls only. Relevant to rear-orchestra occlusion.
6. **NYT's "130 feet"** for the plaster-fall height is inconsistent with a 73–76 ft ceiling.
   Probably the height from the catwalks/grid, or simply wrong. Do not use.
7. **Cost: $84.5M / $86.5M / $88.5M / $90M.** Different scopes (CROH-only vs total).
8. **Reopening 5 vs 6 September 1997.**
9. **Terminology drift.** In the 1993 HSR the 2nd- and 3rd-floor tier is called "dress circle"
   throughout (rooms 204 and 303/304); today the 2nd floor is "Grand Tier" and the 3rd is
   "Dress Circle". The 1965 report already uses "Grand Tier". Any level-name matching across
   documents must account for this.

## 8. GAPS

- **No stated ceiling height above the orchestra floor** in any accessible source.
- **No stated diameter or depth for the recessed ceiling oval.** This is the single biggest
  remaining hole for the acoustic model, since the current `interior_ceiling` dome (r = 26 ft,
  rise 7 ft) is admitted to be illustrative.
- **No tier overhang depths, no box depth, no rail setbacks** published anywhere.
- **No 1997 HVAC / background-noise numbers** (NC ratings, RT before/after). These almost
  certainly exist inside the Paoletti papers.
- No HABS documentation for the Opera House itself was located (only a passing reference in the
  HABS record for the SF Federal Office Building, HABS CA-2866).

## 9. MOST PROMISING PRIMARY DOCUMENTS FOR EXACT DRAWINGS

1. **Arthur Brown Jr. papers, Bancroft Library, UC Berkeley — BANC MSS 81/142 c**, rolls 42/43
   (sheets 1–49, 1931 architectural set) and roll 67 (structural S1–30). The reflected ceiling
   plan and the auditorium longitudinal/transverse sections in this set are the only place the
   **oval's true diameter and depth** and the **ceiling height** will be dimensioned.
   Request scans: bancref-library@berkeley.edu. *(Already identified in the repo; still the
   highest-value unopened source.)*
2. **Carey & Co. HSR 1993, full PDF (14 MB)** —
   https://archive.org/download/historicstructur3199care/historicstructur3199care.pdf
   The narrative is now mined; the *figures* (22, 23, 24 of the main house; 63 typical box
   seating) and the measured floor plans at a single scale are worth re-registering at full
   resolution. Already partly used in `sources/research/hsr1993/`.
3. **1965 SOM/WBE report, full PDF** —
   https://archive.org/download/reportonrehabili1196publ/reportonrehabili1196publ.pdf
   Check its plates for a section; the text alone gives seat spacing, pit critique and HVAC noise.
4. **Paoletti, Graffy, Tedford, Wetherill, JASA 103(5 Suppl.) 2861 (1998), DOI 10.1121/1.421604**
   and the IOA version **DOI 10.25144/20020** ("Opera and Concert Hall Acoustics 1995").
   The IOA proceedings paper is a full paper (the JASA item is a meeting abstract) and is the
   most likely published source for measured RT, background noise and a renovation section
   drawing. Obtain via IOA sales or ILL.
5. **SF DPW Bureau of Architecture 1990s construction drawings** (City & County of San Francisco
   archives). `sources/DATA_ACQUISITION.md` notes a 1997 *Sound and Vibration* article reproduced
   a Bureau of Architecture longitudinal section — tracking that issue down would be a cheap win.
6. **Beranek, *Concert Halls and Opera Houses*, 2nd ed. 2004, Hall 22, pp. 141–144** — plan and
   section at a stated scale, plus the full RT dataset. Archive.org CDL / library copy.
7. **The Architect and Engineer, Nov 1932, vol. 111 no. 2** (archive.org, usmodernist
   AECA-1932-10–1933-03): p. 12 first-floor plan, **p. 16 longitudinal section through auditorium
   and galleries**, p. 17 foyer/basement section, p. 42 structural plan of boxes and balconies.
   A higher-resolution rescan of p. 16 than the current ~9 px/ft halftone would let the ceiling
   oval be measured directly.
8. **San Francisco Public Library, San Francisco History Center** — construction and interior
   photographs, e.g. digitalsf.org islandora:148192 / 148193 / 148197 (1931 views toward stage
   and from the dress circle) and AAD-7661; OpenSFHistory wnp28.0727 (1930s view down into the
   boxes, showing the original projection booth).
